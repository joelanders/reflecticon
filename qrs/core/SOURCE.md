## The PROM images

The plugin holds no PROM bytes. It keeps only:
- each chip's size and SHA-256;
- the first 32 bits of each 128-byte page's SHA-256, enough to say which pages differ but nothing of
  the bytes;
- the SIP's seven repair masks.

`qrs/tests/no_rom_bytes.py` (CI: `.github/workflows/rom-guard.yml`, on every pull request) fails on
any binary file, long hex string or byte table in `qrs/`.

The user supplies three images as binary files: MY (U426), SIP (U508) and DY (U633). At load:
- **The SIP dump.** The only known dump has seven rotted bytes (the project's notes 2026-11-12). The
  plugin recognises it by its hash, flips the seven masks back and checks the repaired chip's hash.
- **A near miss.** If an image differs from the chip only at those seven addresses, the plugin repairs
  them too. At each address the byte may be the chip's, a pure 1 -> 0 loss under the mask, or the
  dump's own pattern (0x540 is not a pure loss). All combinations are tried against the chip's hash,
  and the status line names the addresses repaired.
- **Anything else.** An image that is neither a known chip nor a near miss is used as it is. The
  status line names the pages that differ: SIP groups, MY room tables, or DY pages. Bytes can't be
  named without holding the chip's bytes.
- **Swapped slots.** Images in the wrong slots are put back, by hash or else by contents: DY's level
  table at 0x300 rises from 0 to 0xff, and MY has seven pages of tables with the rest blank. The status
  line says so. Two images that look like the same chip, or a wrong size, are refused with a message
  naming the slots.

The COP controller's two PROMs (card 1: U308 display, U309 microcode) are optional. Without them the
panel is hand-written from the manual. With them the panel is the unit's own: the controller and panel
boards at gate level (`core/cop_panel*.hpp`, project note `notes/2026-11-28-0535-cop-panel-cpp.md`),
checked bit for bit against the project's gate sim on keys, knob, blink, recall, file mode, the
battery RAM, the display and the control bus. Either way the audio settings come from the program
record through `qrs::record_settings`, gate-checked against the COP's microcode.

## How exact it is

- **Digital chain (ADC words in, DAC words out):** bit-exact against the project's Python model, which
  is bit-exact against the project's gate-level simulation of the boards (DY, SIP, MY, DIS, and the
  COP-driven machine). `emu/cmp_cpp.py`, 5 settings cases × 12 000 frames: all identical.
- **Converters:** modelled from the AD and DA sheets at 80 kHz (4 × the 20 kHz frame). The AD front
  end is a bilinear filter plus a 24-tap FIR fitted to the analogue cascade (within −0.31..+0.01 dB,
  phase within 1.5°, up to 10 kHz). Its words can differ from the project's continuous-time model
  near the law's steps: 0.25 % rms of the output on noise.
- **Against a real unit:** two YouTube demos of one QRS. They play a dry synth riff we have (the same
  file, sample for sample) through factory programs 2-8 of files 7 and 8. The input is known, so the
  model gets the same input, with the dry signal subtracted from the clip:
  - **The waveform matches.** Real and model reverb line up within 0.2 ms, with coherence 0.8-0.96
    below 1 kHz in 13 of the 14 programs. In the free tail of file 7's last program coherence is 0.98
    at low frequencies, and in two room-2 programs the 100-1500 Hz waveforms correlate at 0.99. This
    needs the same delays, gains and rounding as the machine.
  - **Levels:** in that free tail the model is within 0.1 dB of the real unit from 125 Hz to 2 kHz and
    within 0.7 dB at 8 kHz, falling with it over 27 dB.
  - **Decay:** in the riff's rests, file 8's room-0 and room-2 programs decay within 5 % of the real
    unit at 250 Hz-1 kHz. File 7's 45 s program decays within about 5 % over its first 30 dB, in the
    same two slopes.
  - **The unit's clock** runs 200 ppm fast against the recording, a crystal tolerance. The model
    matches it only with its clock moved by the same amount.

  That's one unit through a lossy codec, with the uploader's chain measured from the dry pass (flat
  within 1 dB to 8 kHz). Project notes `notes/2026-11-28-0314-riff-ab.md` and
  `notes/2026-11-28-0326-riff-ab-part2.md`.

## Checked (2026-11-26, `qrs_check_machine` against the user's PROM images)

- **Readouts and presets:** readouts for every parameter, and the factory programs' reverb times
  (files 7 and 8) within 6 % of the project's measurements.
- **An impulse through the host:**
  - the reverb delay moves the tail;
  - the 1st reflection lands on the right output at the set time, and 1 -> 200 ms moves it by 199.0 ms;
  - FREEZE holds the tail, MUTE clears it;
  - with the reverb and the reflection both off, outputs 1 and 2 are silent.
- **Changes while a tail rings:**
  - a reverb time change keeps the ring and switches the decay at once;
  - a room change mutes the output for 0.5 s (manual p 15), then the kept memory rings on in the new
    room.

  The manual names no clearing for either.
- **A memory recall from the panel** mutes the output and clears the reverb, also when the room stays
  the same, as both recordings of a real unit show (0.2-0.65 s silences, the earlier tail gone). The
  manual doesn't mention it. It is the hardware: a memory key (or a room-size key, or MUTE) fires the
  COP's Kill one-shot, 0.45 s. With the COP ROMs the plugin runs that; without them it mutes for 0.5 s.
  A host preset change can't be told apart from automation, so it doesn't clear.
- **Extremes, at 10^6 m^3 and 100 s:**
  - with LOW / HIGH at both panel limits, and with the SIP repaired or as dumped, there is no growth
    over 15 s;
  - the 4-bit HIGH / LOW registers at -8 / +7, beyond the panel, decay too (reported, not checked);
  - 1 m^3 at 0.1 s dies away to the floor rounding's idle pattern (0.5 LSB rms).
- **ROM handling, on images made at test time by flipping bits of the user's own:** the dump, the
  chip, partial rot, extra rot, MY/DY swapped, all three moved, two DYs, a 2047-byte image, and MY or DY
  with a byte changed.
- **Host rates:** at 44.1, 48, 88.2, 96 and 192 kHz the reflection lands at the same time after the
  reported latency (within 11 us).
- **Block sizes:** blocks of 1, 4096 and random sizes give the same output as 512-sample blocks, bit
  for bit.
- **The kit's host checks:** no allocation in `process()`, two instances, and a live change without
  allocation.
- **The panel,** hand-written and the COP's: parameter keys, the selected display blinking, the knob
  following the last key, memory recall (`* 8 * 6`: `*` toggles File mode, as on the unit), FREEZE; with
  the COP, the battery RAM saved with the session and restored.
- **Saved state:** the editor shot's state round trip gives the same panel.
- **Validation:** auval `aufx Qr82 Rflx` passed; pluginval at strictness 10 on the VST3 (GUI tests
  skipped): SUCCESS.
- **In a browser** (2026-11-27, Chromium): it boots from dropped files and plays; keys, the knob, memory
  recall and FREEZE work, and the page follows the panel (`web/README.md`).
- **Speed:** the web module boots in Node with 14 presets at 53x real time; the plugin runs at 68x
  real time on one thread (M-series Mac).

## Known differences and open questions

- **Against a real unit, still open** (the A/B above):
  - Per-program levels in file 8: the factory table gives no reverb levels, so presets use 0 dB.
    Matching waveforms then differ by up to 5.5 dB (Ship/submarine), which looks like stored levels.
  - File 8 location 2 (Wardrobe, full) is tilted against the real unit: +5 dB at 125 Hz, -4 dB at
    1 kHz.
  - File 8 location 8 fits no setting, the other factory programs included. The unit's program may
    differ from the manual's table.
- **8 kHz response:** XLR in to XLR out is -5.9 dB at 8 kHz in the model, where the manual's
  specification says -3 dB (the project's note 2026-11-18-0129). The real unit's reverb matches the
  model at 8 kHz within 0.7 dB.
- **Reverb time:** rooms 4-6 decay in two slopes, and the short programs (0.16-0.4 s, rooms 0 and 2)
  measure about 25 % off their labels at 1 kHz. The real unit does both.
- **Reflection routing:** input L's reflection on output 2 follows the manual (p 42), not a traced
  backplane. It reaches outputs 1 / 2 only. The Outputs parameter picks 1 / 2, 3 / 4 (reverb only) or
  their sum. Polarity re-inverts outputs 2 and 4, which the unit leaves inverted.
- **The COP panel, open points:**
  - The blink rate: the oscillator's charging path isn't in the netlist; the plugin uses 0.25 s.
  - The FILE display's point (File mode) isn't driven through the display ROM in the netlist; the
    plugin lights it from the File toggle.
  - Delays and levels under 100 show left-aligned (`45`, `-3` in the first two digits), from the
    display ROM in both sims. Not yet checked against a photo of a real panel.
  - A recall onto a page whose REVERB TIME is outside the previous page's room range rewrites it to
    that range (e.g. 7.7 at 12.5 s, recalled from a room-3 page, is stored back as 10 s): the COP
    clamps against the room it read a cycle earlier. Both sims do this; whether the unit does is open
    (the A/B clip's recalls never cross a range).
  - A new unit's battery RAM: the plugin fills every page with its defaults and files 7-8 with the
    factory table; the real factory contents are not dumped.
- **Not modelled:**
  - ENHANCE's sound: the manual describes seven fixed reflection programs whose circuit isn't traced.
    With the COP its key toggles its LED, as on the unit; without, it is drawn only.
  - Without the COP: copying (`=`) and the user memories. With it they are the COP's own (not yet
    checked against the manual).

  FREEZE, ENTER (the input gate) and MUTE follow the manual. FREEZE is gate-checked in the project,
  switched on mid-sound and off again (297/297 frames identical). The plugin switches it between
  frames, where the hardware switches at the next group boundary: within one frame.
- **DY bit 0 is timing-sensitive** in the gate sim. The rule the model uses holds at nominal and slow
  memory timing. Near the read path's edge (just over one 48.8 ns clock), about 23 % of words take a
  different bit 0. That is ±1 LSB before level scaling, and an open hardware question.
